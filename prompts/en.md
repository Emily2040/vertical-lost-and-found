# The Vertical Lost & Found: English build prompt

This is a consolidated brief for the project built with Gemini 4 in Google Antigravity. It combines the original concept with requirements refined during iterative work. It is not a verbatim transcript of the whole session or a guarantee of identical output.

The prompt describes the target. The included candidate uses constrained character support, analytic mechanisms and discrete cargo handling. Its photographic target remains unmet; full motion, listening, audiovisual sync and human difficulty review remain open. The material sources were generated at 1024 × 1024 and prepared at 2048 × 2048. See [provenance](../docs/provenance.md) and [verification](../docs/verification.md) for the achieved scope.

## Core prompt

```text
Build a climbing puzzle across a monumental wall of drawers inside an abandoned railway hall.

Begin with a drawer bursting open beneath me. Its contents roll into a receiving tray, pulling a counterweight that opens the next drawer just in time for my jump.

Let me climb the mechanism while changing its loads. Pull drawers along rails, latch them at visible notches and transfer substantial objects between connected trays. A drawer can become a step, lift or counterweight depending on how I use it.

Fill the wall with recognisable belongings: trunks, rolled carpets, boxed instruments and ceramic parcels. Model rails, rollers, exposed cables and transfer chutes in Blender. Use Nano Banana for handled lacquer, luggage canvas and worn lining.

Stage one builds a staircase from three drawers. Stage two moves one heavy case between linked drawers to raise a lift while preserving a return route. Stage three requires an ascending sequence of load transfers, moving platforms and timed jumps.

Preserve object identity and mass. My weight must affect an unlocked platform, and latches must engage real notches.

Finish by opening the final drawer from inside. Daylight reveals a rooftop garden beyond the colossal storage wall. Capture the complete progression in an energetic one-minute showcase.
```

## Expanded build brief

Copy the text below into your build session:

```text
Build THE VERTICAL LOST & FOUND, a complete playable Three.js climbing puzzle inside a cavernous railway hall. A monumental wall of drawers holds the belongings of an absent city. Begin with a drawer bursting open beneath the player: a rolling parcel tips a receiving tray, and its cable hauls another drawer outward to catch the next jump. Every visible cause must produce the corresponding gameplay consequence.

Give the archive architectural weight: deep structural bays, oxblood lacquered drawers, worn canvas luggage, muted crimson felt, tarnished brass hardware, rollers, notched rails, taut cables and counterweight cages. Use recognisable possessions: a trunk, rolled carpet, ceramic parcel, umbrella case and boxed instruments. Vary cabinet proportions and wear with purpose. Keep footholds, cargo routes and selected controls readable against the background. Give the character credible proportions, weight-bearing poses and visible contact with steps and controls. Finish by opening the last drawer from inside, revealing a sunlit rooftop garden beyond the enormous storage wall.

Make the player rearrange loads to climb. Drawers slide along constrained rails, lock at visible notches and serve as steps, lifts or counterweights according to their position and load. Opening a cargo hatch permits a valid transfer; exposed cables explain coupled movement. Preserve each object's identity and mass throughout pickup, placement and chute travel. Character weight must affect an unlocked loaded support. Reject missed notches, unreachable interactions and impossible transfers with an understandable reason.

Build three distinct stages. First, create a staircase using three drawers and one movable case. Next, transfer that load between linked drawers to raise a lift while preserving a return ledge. Finally, climb a taller route with a limited set of objects, dispatching loads down chutes so platforms arrive where and when they are needed. Increase planning, load-management and timing demands through the mechanisms. Provide a recoverable checkpoint before each commitment, clear objectives and progress, meaningful failure and a reliable retry. The garden ending must be earned through the working route.

Verify access to the actual tools before production: Gemini 4 in the selected environment, Nano Banana image generation, Blender background mode with bpy, Three.js, browser interaction and inspection, capture, FFmpeg and capable motion/audio review. Record the available versions and exact model/tool identifiers; mark identifiers unavailable when the service does not expose them. Use relevant installed authoring and review skills. If a required capability is unavailable, record the blocked requirement and continue independent work. Describing a future tool call does not fulfil it. Where subagents are available, assign bounded asset, runtime and independent-review work, with one integrator owning shared interfaces and visual decisions. Otherwise perform those roles sequentially.

Keep project memory in schema-validated files. contract.json defines metres, seconds, kilograms, axes, names and interfaces. world.json holds stable entity IDs, parent relationships, coordinate-tagged transforms, asset revisions, material assignments, camera/light presets and settled art decisions. progress.json tracks owners, milestones, unresolved work, next actions and evidence. Keep transient simulation state separate. Builders, exporters and loaders must consume or validate these records, including Blender-to-Three.js coordinate conversion. Reconstruct the project from saved files without relying on conversation history.

Save an art-direction reference and a target hero view before scaling production. Complete one representative drawer from generated material imagery through Blender to the browser first. Model real thickness, bevels, rails, rollers, latches and cable attachments in headless Blender; keep moving parts separate with correct pivots and explicit roles. Geometry must provide silhouette, parallax and contact where the camera resolves them. Save editable .blend sources and repeatable build/export scripts.

Actually invoke Nano Banana to generate production texture imagery for handled lacquer, luggage canvas and worn interior lining. Save untouched outputs under assets/textures/raw/ and prepared maps under assets/textures/pbr/. Generate neutral-lit, perspective-free surface imagery with suitable coverage and scale. Inspect the actual outputs for baked lighting, seams, invented marks and oversized grain or fibres; repair or regenerate failures. Produce exact drawer numbers, weight markings and gameplay symbols with deterministic text or decals.

Apply the generated imagery to UV-mapped Blender meshes. Prepare at least 2048-pixel material maps for the hero surfaces, recording native dimensions and every crop or resize; enlargement alone does not establish detail. Author or bake roughness, metalness and normal information for their physical roles. Identify approximate maps honestly. Arbitrary colour brightness is not measured PBR data. Record successful prompts, reference inputs, output hashes, native dimensions, preparation steps, mesh IDs, UV sets, material slots and channel assignments in assets/textures/provenance.json. Procedural materials may supplement this work.

Export GLB and inspect the same asset in Three.js. Preserve texture transforms, colour spaces, normal orientation and material assignments. Save matched Blender/browser close-ups, a clay or wireframe view and a material-isolation view. Compare silhouette, bevel highlights, fibre scale, seams, roughness, contact shadows and wear placement against the saved target. Correct a failed representative asset before multiplying it across the hall. Inspect the finished scene at 1920 × 1080, including the character, hardest-stage camera and garden reveal. Improvement over an earlier weak version is not sufficient acceptance.

Declare which behaviours use rigid bodies, constrained kinematics, analytic equations or game approximations. Use one authoritative mechanism state and a fixed simulation step. Keep rendered floors, rims, load pans, cargo bottoms and chute geometry consistent with support/contact calculations. Preserve moving-platform velocity when riding and jumping. Test balanced and unbalanced loads, character weight, pressure conditions, latch release, impossible transfers, cargo jams and recovery, joint limits, maximum-speed contacts, frame-time spikes, render-rate independence and resets while parts are moving. Save expected versus measured results with scene-scale tolerances. Inspect actual rendered contacts as well as numeric assertions.

Replay all three stages through ordinary controls. Automation must obey the same reach, movement, load and checkpoint rules as the player. Make play decisions from player-visible information; use hidden state only for diagnostics and assertions. Demonstrate failure, retry and the hardest ascent without teleporting, unlocking stages through test hooks or requiring superhuman timing. Keep human difficulty testing separate from a bot's successful run. Have an independent reviewer inspect the route, visual target and source-specific tests; fix consequential defects and rerun affected checks.

Deliver showcase-1080p60.mp4: target exactly 60.000 seconds, 3,600 frames, progressive 1920 × 1080 at constant 60 FPS. Show the opening drawer catch, a readable load transfer, increasing challenge, the hardest ascent and the earned garden ending. Frame the mechanics so a new viewer understands what caused each movement. Keep loading screens, consoles and debug overlays out. Map demonstrated features to timestamps and keep edge-case test evidence in the acceptance report.

Qualify picture and sound with a short capture before recording the full minute. Prefer measured real-time capture when it sustains the required output. If that is infeasible, capture the actual application deterministically at defined simulation times with synchronised application audio, and disclose the offline method. Measure runtime performance separately using distinct application frames, without counting polling callbacks or renderer passes as extra frames. Do not manufacture 60 FPS through duplicated low-rate footage or interpolation. Capture one complete attempt into a fresh folder; retain its input trace, frame records, source/build hashes and original frames. Preserve interrupted attempts separately.

Export high-quality H.264 MP4 with yuv420p, correctly converted and tagged SDR Rec.709, and 48 kHz stereo AAC. Use the application's original or rights-cleared sound, retain mechanical detail, and exclude unrelated microphone or desktop audio. Measure loudness and aim for a true peak at or below −1 dBTP. Check clipping, dropouts, echo and event timing. Validate the exact encoded file's duration, decoded frame count, timestamps, clean decode, audio streams and endpoints. Watch the entire video at normal speed and listen end to end, checking sync near the start, middle and end. Sampled stills, metadata and waveforms cannot clear those reviews.

Deliver the runnable application, editable Blender scenes, raw/generated and prepared assets, provenance, repeatable build scripts, shared JSON records, setup and control instructions, saved replays, performance evidence and the final MP4. Include a delivery verifier that checks actual saved outputs and fails for missing files, broken references, failed mechanics or nonconforming media. Tie tests and capture to the delivered revision. Report each requirement as passed, failed, blocked or unreviewed. Separate technical verification from visual, motion, listening and human-playability judgement. If required review is unavailable or the visual target still fails, deliver a clearly labelled candidate with the remaining work; keep the project incomplete. Preserve the full mechanism, three-stage progression and visual ambition throughout iteration, within the authorised resource budget.
```
